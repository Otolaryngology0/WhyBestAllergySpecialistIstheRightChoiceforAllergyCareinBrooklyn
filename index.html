Practical Tips for Better Allergy Relief

  • Know Your Triggers:

    • Keeping a simple diary of when symptoms occur, where you were, and what you were doing can reveal patterns. Pollen counts, weather changes, cleaning products, and pets are common culprits. Share this diary with your allergist, as it helps guide testing and treatment.

  • Reduce Allergens at Home:

    • Wash bedding weekly in hot water, use allergen-proof covers on pillows and mattresses, keep humidity under control to limit mold, vacuum regularly with a filter designed to trap fine particles, and keep pets out of the bedroom if pet dander is a trigger. Small habits add up to meaningful allergy relief over time.

  • Plan for High-Pollen Days:

    • During peak pollen seasons, try to keep windows closed, shower and change clothes after spending time outdoors, and avoid hanging laundry outside. If you take medication for seasonal allergies, many patients find it helpful to discuss the best timing with their allergist before the season begins.

  • Protect Your Eyes:

    • If you suffer from itchy eyes, avoid rubbing them, remove contact lenses if they feel irritating, and rinse with clean water or sterile saline. If symptoms persist or your vision changes, seek professional care without delay.

  • Never Ignore Severe Reactions:

    • Anaphylaxis is a serious allergic reaction that can involve trouble breathing, swelling of the face or throat, dizziness, a rapid heartbeat, or widespread hives. This is a medical emergency. Call 911 immediately, and use your prescribed emergency medication if you have been given one. Talk with an allergist about an action plan if you are at risk.

  • Keep Follow-Up Appointments:

    • Allergy care works best when it is ongoing. Regular follow-up visits allow your allergist to check your progress, adjust your plan, and answer new questions as your needs change.
